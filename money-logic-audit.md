| 12 | Loan interest | **Flat rate on original principal**, not reducing-balance: `totalInterest = principal * flatRatePercent/100`; `totalRepayable = principal + totalInterest`; `monthlyInstallment = round2(totalRepayable / tenorMonths)`. Rounding: `monthlyInstallment × tenorMonths` does **not** always equal `totalRepayable` to the pesewa (e.g. principal 1000, rate 10%, 7 months → `totalRepayable=1100.00`, `monthlyInstallment=round2(1100/7)=157.14`, `157.14×7=1099.98`, a 2‑pesewa **shortfall**) — see Gap #6. | `lib/loan.ts:7-17` |
| 13 | Loan repayment | Each repayment is recorded as **one lump `amount`** (`loan_repayments.amount`) — there is no principal/interest split column on the row itself. This is the textbook-correct simplification for a **flat-rate** loan (interest doesn't compound or shrink with balance the way it would on a reducing-balance loan, so a fixed proportional split per payment is appropriate here — not a bug). `record_loan_repayment()` decrements `current_balance` by the full `amount` (principal+interest together) and auto-flips the loan to `completed` once `current_balance <= 0`. | `0023_collected_interest_rpc.sql:4-20`; `0007_loan_rpcs.sql:55-114` |

See Gap #3. | `components/loan-status-control.tsx:14-18, 43-52` |
| 15 | Reporting — revenue/income/profit | **Single source of truth**: `computeAccountSummary()` in `lib/finance/account-summary.ts`. Current exact composition (as of this audit): `totalRevenue = round2((interest?loanInterest:0) + (commission?commission:0) + (susu_fees?susuFees:0) + (card_fees?cardFees:0) + (sms_fees?totalSmsFees:0) + (processing_fees?processingFees:0) − consolidatedFundDeposits)`, gated per-component by admin-configurable toggles (all six currently enabled by default). `loanInterest` = **collected** interest only (`compute_collected_loan_interest()`, proportional-split method above) — **not** scheduled/expected interest; a disbursed-but-unpaid loan contributes zero to revenue until repayments arrive. Nothing prevents a loan from being activated even if it would leave the company's cash at bank/hand below what it owes depositors — the only gate on `activate_loan()` is the processing-fee-account-balance check (#11), unrelated to liquidity. | grep across `lib/`, `app/(dashboard)`, `supabase/migrations` for `cover`, `reserve`, `lendable`, `liquidity` — no matches |

No check anywhere that `Σ(individual client balances) = a control total`. | `app/api/reconciliation/route.ts:1-69`; `app/(dashboard)/reconciliation/page.tsx:53-114` (derive/rendering — no ledger cross-check) |

## D. The three fixes that matter most, in order

**1. Recognize an expense when savings interest is disbursed (Gap #1).**
This is first because it is the only finding that silently, permanently, and invisibly overstates the single number the owner is told to trust most — Account Balance — with no natural mechanism to ever self-correct. Every other issue in this report is either bounded (a specific transaction type, a specific edge case) or requires a specific trigger (a network retry, a specific rounding remainder); this one fires on every single use of a legitimate, presumably-routine feature (paying interest to good clients), and the error only grows over time. Fix: when `disburse_interest()` credits an account, also write a row to `expenditures` (or a dedicated interest-expense ledger) for the same amount, and add an `interestPaid` term (subtracted) to `computeAccountSummary()`'s `accountBalance` formula.

**2. Add write-off logic for defaulted loans (Gap #3).**
Second because, like #1, it's a standing liability-side blind spot that compounds silently over time and currently has *zero* code path to correct it — a defaulted loan's principal sits in "money we're owed" forever. Unlike #1 it requires a specific event (a default) to trigger, which is why it ranks below #1, but a microfinance book that never writes off bad debt will eventually show a materially fictitious asset position. Fix: a proper `write_off_loan()` RPC — server-validated status transition, zeroes or flags `current_balance`, inserts a bad-debt expense — replacing the current bare `UPDATE` from the client.

**3. Fix the day-1 susu early-exit total loss (Gap #4).**
Third because, unlike #1 and #2, this doesn't corrupt any company-wide reported figure — it's bounded to one client, one bad day, one wrong outcome. But it's the most *concrete, specification-violating* bug in the report (the reference model calls out this exact scenario by name), it's a direct harm to an actual client rather than an abstract reporting error, and it's cheap to fix: change the penalty calculation to `min(daily_contribution_amount, total_collected)` capped so it can never fully zero out a payout below some floor, or explicitly special-case day 1 to a full refund as specified.

*(Gaps #2, #5, #6, #7 are all real and worth fixing, but are lower-frequency, lower-blast-radius, or purely structural risks rather than active, ongoing corruption of a reported figure — they didn't make the top three for that reason, not because they're unimportant.)*

## E. Things the code does that this reference model doesn't cover

- **Consolidated Fund account (SAV-00079)** — a company-owned "savings" account that formally receives the day's revenue after 20:30, DB-enforced by time. This is PFS-specific machinery for moving already-earned revenue into a segregated account; the reference model has no equivalent concept.
- **Transaction edit/reversal with downstream `bal_after` recompute** — admin can edit or soft-delete (reverse) a deposit/withdrawal, and the system walks every later transaction on that account forward to keep every historical snapshot internally consistent. Not addressed by the reference model at all.
- **Telecom mobile-money mini-app** — an entirely separate flat transaction log (`telecom_transactions`) with its own revenue calc (`lib/finance/telecom-summary.ts`), deliberately walled off from every figure in this audit. Out of scope for this reference model by design (confirmed in-repo: `telecom-mini-app-brief.md §7`), not a gap.
- **Staff/admin account deletion cascades into transaction history** — deleting a staff member's login also *hard-deletes* every transaction, susu payment, and bank transaction they personally recorded (with dependent totals recalculated afterward so balances stay internally consistent). This is a deliberate, documented choice, not an accident — but it does mean real client-facing deposit/withdrawal history can permanently disappear from a client's account statement if their recording staff member is later deleted. Worth knowing even though it's not a "wrong number" bug.
- **Per-transaction `bal_after` snapshot on every row** — a running-balance audit trail baked into the ledger itself, beyond what the reference model asks for.
- **Interest disbursement pinned to a historical balance snapshot** (`balance_as_of()`), specifically to prevent a client's later withdrawal/top-up from retroactively changing what they're owed for having held a qualifying balance through the window — a more careful design than the reference model's simple "interest owed" concept implies is needed.

## Bottom line

The transaction-ledger core (deposits, withdrawals, commission, card fees, SMS fees, susu contributions) is **built correctly** — properly separated liability vs. income, correctly cash-basis, correctly reversal-safe, and the Account Balance formula's treatment of fee add-backs is genuinely well thought out, matching the reference model's invariant almost exactly. The real damage in this audit is concentrated in exactly two places that never got the same rigor: **interest paid to clients** (Gap #1) and **loan write-offs** (Gap #3) — both are one-directional liability/asset movements with no corresponding expense ever recorded, so both silently drift the company's reported cash position further from reality every time they're used. Neither is a subtle rounding issue; both are a fully missing accounting entry.
